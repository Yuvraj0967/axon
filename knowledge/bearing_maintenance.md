# Bearing maintenance notes

Project-authored general guidance for the repair agent. It is not manufacturer documentation. Where a machine's own manual disagrees, the manual wins. A technician confirms every diagnosis.

## Before replacing a bearing

A vibration warning means the machine behaves differently from its healthy baseline. It does not prove the bearing is damaged. Check first for loose mounting bolts, a worn or misaligned coupling, belt tension, shaft imbalance and a changed load. These produce similar vibration and cost less to fix.

## Inner race damage

Damage on the inner race rotates with the shaft, so the vibration changes with load zone and often shows as sharp, repeating knocks. Inspect the shaft journal for fretting or creep, check the fit, and replace the bearing and inspect the shaft seat. Running on a bearing with inner race damage risks shaft damage, so schedule the visit soon.

## Outer race damage

Outer race damage sits in the housing and stays at a fixed position, so knocks repeat steadily. Inspect the housing bore for wear and the bearing for brinelling from shock loads or misalignment. Replace the bearing and verify the housing fit.

## Rolling element damage

Damaged balls or rollers produce irregular knocks that vary more between windows. Look for lubricant contamination, corrosion from moisture and incorrect preload. Replace the whole bearing; do not reuse one with damaged rolling elements.

## Lubrication

Too little, too much or contaminated grease raises heat and vibration. Use the grease type and quantity in the machine manual. Over-greasing can overheat a sealed bearing. Record the date and amount whenever grease is added.

## What the technician should bring

The part number for the bearing on each side, a matching seal, the correct grease, and tools for pulling and fitting the bearing without hammering the races. Ask the owner for the machine model and the idle window so the visit does not interrupt production.

## After the repair

Run the machine at normal load and record a new vibration baseline once it settles. Tell AXON the finding (fault confirmed, repaired or no fault found) so results from before the visit are not counted towards a new warning.
